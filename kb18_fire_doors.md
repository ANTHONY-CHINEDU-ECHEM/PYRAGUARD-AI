## What compartmentation does

Buildings are divided into compartments by fire resisting walls, floors and doors. The purpose is to hold fire and smoke in the place where they start for long enough for people to escape and for firefighters to work. A fire door marked FD30 is designed to resist fire for 30 minutes and an FD60 door for 60 minutes. The letter S after the rating means the door also has seals that restrict the passage of cold smoke.

## How fire doors protect escape routes

Corridors and stairways used as escape routes are protected by fire doors so that they stay free of smoke. A fire door only works when it is closed. A single door wedged open can allow smoke to fill a stairway and cut off everyone above the fire.

## Actions during a fire

* Close doors behind you as you leave each room and corridor.
* Make sure doors held open on automatic hold open devices have released when the alarm sounded, and close them by hand if they have not.
* Keep stairway doors closed so the stair stays clear for people coming down and firefighters going up.
* Report to the fire and rescue service any fire door or shutter that failed to close.

## Do not

* Do not wedge or prop fire doors open.
* Do not block the swing of a fire door with stock, furniture or waste.
* Do not open a fire door to look at the fire.

## Maintenance

Fire doors should be checked regularly for damaged leaves and frames, missing or painted over seals, faulty closers and excessive gaps. Service penetrations through compartment walls and floors must be fire stopped, because unsealed holes for cables and pipes are a common route for smoke spread.
