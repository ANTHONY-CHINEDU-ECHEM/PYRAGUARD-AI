## Why verification matters

Most automatic fire alarm signals from commercial premises turn out to be false alarms caused by cooking fumes, steam, dust, aerosols or faults. They disrupt business, make people slow to respond to real alarms and tie up fire engines. For that reason several UK fire and rescue services no longer send an automatic response to an alarm signal from many non residential premises unless a fire has been confirmed by a person, typically through a 999 call.

## What counts as confirmation

A fire is confirmed when a person has seen flame or smoke, smelled burning, or seen a credible picture of the fire. A camera view showing flame or smoke at the alarm location is strong confirmation and should be passed to the fire and rescue service in the 999 call. Operation of a manual call point, a sprinkler flow switch or more than one detector also makes a real fire much more likely.

## Verification actions

* Check the camera covering the alarm location as soon as an alarm or a video alert is raised.
* Call 999 immediately when flame or smoke is seen on camera or reported by a person, and say that the fire is confirmed.
* Send a warden to investigate an unconfirmed alarm within the investigation time set by the fire strategy, with a radio and a clear exit.
* Treat the alarm as real and evacuate if the cause has not been found by the end of the investigation time.
* Log the cause of every false alarm and correct it.

## Do not

* Do not delay calling 999 when there are any signs of fire.
* Do not silence the alarm and assume a false alarm without checking the location.
* Do not send a person alone into a zone where a second detector has also operated.

## Design measures

Multi sensor detectors, correct detector choice for each room, coincidence detection (two detectors before full alarm) and staff alarms with a short investigation period all reduce false alarms without delaying the response to a real fire.
