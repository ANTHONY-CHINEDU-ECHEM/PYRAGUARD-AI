## Why smoke is the main threat

In United Kingdom fire statistics the most common cause of death in fires is being overcome by gas or smoke, not burns. Smoke contains carbon monoxide and, where plastics and foams are burning, hydrogen cyanide. A few breaths can cause confusion and collapse. Smoke is hot and rises, spreads sideways under the ceiling, then banks down towards the floor. It travels through open doors, stairwells, ceiling voids and ducts far faster than flame, so a room well away from the fire can become deadly.

## Actions when smoke is present

* Treat visible smoke as a fire and raise the alarm, even if no flame can be seen.
* Leave by a route that is clear of smoke and choose an alternative exit if your usual route is smoky.
* Keep low where the air is cleaner if you must pass through light smoke to reach an exit.
* Close every door behind you to hold the smoke back.
* Check a closed door with the back of your hand before opening it, and do not open it if it is warm.

## Actions if you are trapped

* Go into a room with a window and close the door.
* Block the gap under the door with clothing, towels or bedding to keep smoke out.
* Call 999 and state exactly where you are in the building.
* Open the window and stay near it for fresh air, and signal so that firefighters can see you.

## Do not

* Do not enter a smoke filled corridor or stairwell to reach an exit: turn back and find another way.
* Do not prop fire doors open, because an open door lets smoke fill the escape route.
* Do not open a door that feels warm.
* Do not re enter a smoky area to look for other people: report them missing at the assembly point.

## Smouldering fires

A smouldering fire can produce large volumes of toxic smoke with little or no visible flame. Smoke alone is enough reason to evacuate and call the fire and rescue service.
