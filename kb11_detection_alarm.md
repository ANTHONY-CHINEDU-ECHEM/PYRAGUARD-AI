## System categories

BS 5839 Part 1 groups fire alarm systems by purpose. Category M systems are manual only and rely on people operating call points. Category L systems protect life with automatic detection, graded from L1 (detection throughout the building) down to L5 (detection designed for a specific risk). Category P systems protect property, with P1 covering the whole building and P2 covering defined high risk areas. The category for a building comes from its fire risk assessment.

## Detector technologies

Point optical smoke detectors respond to visible smoke particles and suit most offices and corridors. Heat detectors respond to a fixed temperature or a rapid rate of rise and are used where smoke detectors would give false alarms, such as kitchens. Multi sensor detectors combine smoke, heat and sometimes carbon monoxide sensing to reject false alarms. Beam detectors cover large open spaces. Aspirating systems draw air through sampling pipes to a very sensitive detector and give very early warning in server rooms and cold stores. Flame detectors sense the ultraviolet or infrared radiation of flame.

## Where video based detection helps

Ceiling mounted detectors only respond once smoke or heat reaches them. In tall spaces such as warehouses and atria, smoke can cool and stratify below the roof, which delays detection. A camera sees flame and smoke at the source, wherever it is in view, and gives the operator a picture to verify. Video detection should be treated as a supplement that adds early warning and visual verification. It does not replace a fire alarm system designed and maintained to BS 5839 Part 1.

## Testing and maintenance actions

* Test the fire alarm every week by operating a manual call point, using a different call point each week.
* Have the system inspected and serviced by a competent person at intervals of no more than six months.
* Record every test, fault, false alarm and service visit in the fire alarm log book.
* Investigate every false alarm and act on the cause.

## Do not

* Do not cover, disable or isolate detectors without a written permit and a compensating measure such as a fire watch.
* Do not silence or reset the alarm until the cause has been found.
