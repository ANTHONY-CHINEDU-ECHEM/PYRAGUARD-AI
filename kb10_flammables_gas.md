## Nature of the hazard

Flammable liquids give off vapour that ignites easily and can flash back to the container. Flammable gases form explosive mixtures with air. Any gas cylinder heated by a fire, including cylinders of non flammable gas, can rupture violently. Acetylene cylinders are a special case because the gas can continue to decompose inside the cylinder after the fire is out.

## Immediate actions

* Raise the alarm, evacuate and call the fire and rescue service.
* Shut off the gas supply at the emergency control valve or cylinder valve if this can be done safely.
* Use foam, dry powder or carbon dioxide on a small flammable liquid fire if you are trained and have a clear exit.
* Keep everyone well away from cylinders that have been exposed to fire or heat.
* Tell the fire and rescue service the location, number and contents of all gas cylinders and flammable stores, and whether acetylene is present.
* Stop spilled liquid reaching drains if this can be done without risk.

## Do not

* Do not extinguish a burning gas leak unless the gas supply can be shut off first: unburnt gas will build up and may explode.
* Never use a water jet on a burning flammable liquid, because it spreads the fire.
* Do not approach, move or cool cylinders that have been heated by fire: leave them to the fire and rescue service.
* Do not operate electrical switches where a gas leak is suspected.

## Storage and legal duty

The Dangerous Substances and Explosive Atmospheres Regulations 2002 require employers to assess and control fire and explosion risks from dangerous substances. Quantities in the workroom should be kept to a minimum, bulk stock should be kept in a dedicated fire resisting store or outdoors, and ignition sources must be excluded.
