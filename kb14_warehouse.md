## Nature of the hazard

Warehouses hold a large fire load in a single open volume. Cardboard, timber pallets, plastic wrap and foam packaging ignite easily, and the vertical flues between racked pallets carry flame to the top of the racking in a very short time. Collapsing stock and racking can block aisles. In a tall building smoke may cool and spread out below the roof, so ceiling detectors can respond late while conditions at floor level still look clear.

## Immediate actions

* Raise the alarm and call the fire and rescue service as soon as flame or smoke is seen among stored goods.
* Evacuate the warehouse by the nearest safe exit, moving away from the affected aisle.
* Stop forklift trucks, park them clear of aisles and exits, and leave on foot.
* Leave the sprinkler system running and make sure the sprinkler control valve stays open.
* Close fire shutters and doors between the warehouse and other parts of the building if they have not closed automatically.
* Tell the fire and rescue service what is stored in the affected area, including aerosols, flammable liquids, gas cylinders and batteries.

## Do not

* Do not shut the sprinkler valve until the fire and rescue service confirms the fire is out.
* Do not try to tackle a fire that has reached racked or stacked goods: it is already beyond a portable extinguisher.
* Do not enter aisles beside burning racking, because stock and racking may collapse.
* Do not use a forklift truck to move burning goods.

## Prevention

Keep the clear space below sprinkler heads that the system design requires. Keep aisles, exits and fire shutter lines clear. Store aerosols and flammable liquids in dedicated protected areas. Separate battery charging from combustible storage, and control hot work with a permit system.
