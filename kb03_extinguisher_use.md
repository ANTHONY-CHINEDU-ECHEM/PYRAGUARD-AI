## When first aid firefighting is acceptable

Portable extinguishers are for small fires in their very first stage. Tackling a fire is only reasonable when every one of the following is true: the alarm has already been raised, the fire is small (a common rule of thumb is no larger than a waste paper bin), the correct extinguisher is to hand, the person has been trained to use it, and there is a clear escape route behind them. If any of these is not true, the right action is to leave and close the door.

## Operating actions

* Raise the alarm before you pick up an extinguisher.
* Keep your back to a clear exit so that the fire is never between you and your way out.
* Pull the safety pin to break the tamper seal.
* Aim the nozzle or horn at the base of the fire, not at the flames.
* Squeeze the handle to discharge the agent.
* Sweep from side to side across the base of the fire until it is out.
* Back away while watching the fire area, because fires can flare up again.
* Leave immediately and close the door if the fire is not out when the extinguisher is empty.

## Do not

* Do not continue if the fire grows, if smoke begins to fill the room or if you feel heat on your face: leave at once.
* Do not hold the horn of a carbon dioxide extinguisher unless it is a frost free type, because the horn becomes cold enough to cause a freeze burn.
* Do not discharge carbon dioxide in a small unventilated space and remain there, because the gas displaces oxygen.
* Do not put a used extinguisher back on its bracket: report it so that it can be recharged or replaced.

## Limits of an extinguisher

A typical portable extinguisher discharges for well under a minute. It will not control a fire that has spread beyond the item where it started. Staff should never feel obliged to fight a fire.
