## Who is responsible

In England and Wales the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 places fire safety duties on the responsible person. That is the employer for a workplace, and otherwise the person who has control of the premises, such as the owner, landlord or managing agent. Where more than one responsible person shares a building they must cooperate and coordinate. Scotland has equivalent duties under the Fire (Scotland) Act 2005 and Northern Ireland under the Fire and Rescue Services (Northern Ireland) Order 2006.

## Core duties

The responsible person must carry out a suitable and sufficient fire risk assessment and keep it under review. They must put in place general fire precautions: means of detecting fire and giving warning, means of escape, emergency lighting and signs, firefighting equipment, and an emergency plan. They must give staff information, instruction and training, appoint competent people to assist, and keep fire safety equipment maintained in efficient working order.

## Recording requirements

Since 1 October 2023, changes made by section 156 of the Building Safety Act 2022 require the responsible person to record the fire risk assessment in full and to record the fire safety arrangements, whatever the size of the business. Before that date a written record was only required in certain cases, such as where five or more people were employed.

## Compliance actions

* Record the complete fire risk assessment and review it regularly and after any fire, near miss or significant change.
* Keep a written emergency plan and make sure every member of staff knows it.
* Keep records of alarm tests, emergency lighting tests, extinguisher servicing, drills and training.
* Share fire safety information with other responsible persons in the same building.

## Enforcement

The local fire and rescue authority enforces the Order in most premises. Inspectors can issue alterations, enforcement and prohibition notices. Serious breaches that put people at risk of death or serious injury are criminal offences punishable by unlimited fines and imprisonment.
