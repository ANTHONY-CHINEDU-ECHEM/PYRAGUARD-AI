## Principle

Getting everyone out is the duty of the responsible person for the premises, not of the fire and rescue service. A plan that depends on firefighters carrying people out is not an acceptable plan. Anyone who cannot leave unaided and quickly, whether through a mobility, sensory or cognitive impairment or a temporary condition, should have a personal emergency evacuation plan, usually called a PEEP. Visitors are covered by a generic emergency evacuation plan.

## Assisted evacuation actions

* Send the named assistants to each person with a personal emergency evacuation plan as soon as the alarm sounds.
* Move the person to the nearest refuge if they cannot use the stairs. A refuge is a protected space, normally on a stair landing, with a two way emergency voice communication point.
* Use the refuge communication point to tell the control point who is waiting and where.
* Use an evacuation chair, or an evacuation lift where one is provided, operated by trained staff.
* Alert people with hearing impairments by a visual beacon, a vibrating pager or a buddy.
* Guide people with visual impairments by offering an arm and describing the route.
* Tell the fire and rescue service on arrival if anyone is still waiting in a refuge, and exactly which refuge.

## Do not

* Do not leave a person alone in a refuge without telling the control point.
* Do not attempt to carry a person down stairs unless that is the method agreed in their plan and the helpers are trained for it.
* Do not use an ordinary passenger lift.

## Planning notes

Refuges are temporary waiting spaces and not a final place of safety. Plans should be rehearsed with the person concerned, reviewed when their needs or workplace change, and should name deputies so that an assistant is always present.
