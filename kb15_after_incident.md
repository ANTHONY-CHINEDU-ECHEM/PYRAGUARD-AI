## Casualty care actions

* Cool a burn with cool or lukewarm running water for 20 minutes as soon as possible.
* Remove clothing and jewellery near the burn unless it is stuck to the skin.
* Cover the cooled burn loosely with cling film or a clean non fluffy dressing.
* Get medical assessment for anyone who has breathed smoke, even if they feel well, because the effects can be delayed.
* Call 999 for anyone with breathing difficulty, burns to the face or airway, large or deep burns, or reduced consciousness.

## Re entry and recovery actions

* Keep everyone at the assembly point until the fire and rescue service officer in charge confirms the building is safe.
* Have the fire alarm, emergency lighting and any suppression system checked and restored by a competent person before the building is occupied again.
* Replace or recharge every extinguisher that has been used.
* Preserve the scene and keep camera footage, alarm logs and system records for the investigation.
* Review the fire risk assessment and the emergency plan in the light of what happened.

## Do not

* Do not apply ice, iced water, creams or greasy substances to a burn.
* Do not burst blisters.
* Do not allow anyone back into the building until the fire and rescue service has declared it safe.
* Do not reset the fire alarm panel until the cause of the alarm has been identified.

## Reporting

Under the Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013, certain work related injuries and certain dangerous occurrences, including some fires and explosions that stop normal work, must be reported to the Health and Safety Executive. Insurers should be told promptly. A short debrief with wardens and staff while memories are fresh is the best source of improvements.
