## Nature of the hazard

Cooking oils and fats burn at very high temperatures and are classed as Class F. If water touches burning oil it turns instantly to steam, expands enormously and throws burning oil out of the pan as a fireball. Grease deposits in extraction canopies and ducts can carry fire out of the kitchen and through the building unseen.

## Immediate actions

* Raise the alarm and call the fire and rescue service.
* Turn off the heat source using the emergency gas shut off or the electrical isolator, if it can be reached safely.
* Operate the manual release of the fixed kitchen fire suppression system where one is fitted.
* Smother a small pan fire with a fire blanket, placing it gently over the pan with your hands shielded behind the blanket, and leave it in place until the pan is cold.
* Use a wet chemical extinguisher on a fryer or larger oil fire if you are trained, applying it gently so the oil is not splashed.
* Leave the kitchen and close the door if the fire is not out at once.
* Tell the fire and rescue service if fire may have entered the extraction duct, even when the pan fire appears to be out.

## Do not

* Never put water on burning oil or fat.
* Do not move or carry a burning pan.
* Do not use a carbon dioxide, water or foam extinguisher on burning oil: the jet can blow or float burning oil out of the pan.
* Do not lift a fire blanket to check the pan until it has fully cooled, because the oil can reignite.
* Do not switch the extraction fan on or leave it running if fire has entered the duct.

## Prevention

Never leave cooking unattended, keep fryer thermostats maintained, and have extraction canopies, filters and ducts cleaned on a schedule that matches how heavily the kitchen is used.
