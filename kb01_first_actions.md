## Purpose

This procedure sets out what any person should do in the first minute after finding a fire or seeing smoke in a workplace. Life safety comes before property. The order matters: warn others, summon help, get out.

## Immediate actions

* Raise the alarm at once by operating the nearest manual call point, even if the fire looks small.
* Call the fire and rescue service on 999 (112 also works) and give the full address, what is burning and whether anyone is missing.
* Leave the building by the nearest safe exit and follow the green running man escape signs.
* Close doors behind you as you leave, if it is safe to do so, to slow the spread of fire and smoke.
* Go straight to the assembly point and report to the fire warden so that everyone can be accounted for.
* Tell the fire and rescue service on arrival where the fire is and what hazards are nearby.

## Do not

* Do not stop to collect personal belongings.
* Do not use lifts, unless a lift is a designated evacuation lift operated under the site plan.
* Do not go back into the building until the fire and rescue service says it is safe.
* Do not try to fight a fire unless you are trained, the fire is small and your escape route is clear behind you.

## Why this order

A fire can double in size in well under a minute once it has taken hold of ordinary furnishings or packaging. Raising the alarm first protects everyone else in the building, including people who cannot see or smell the fire yet. Calling the fire and rescue service early costs nothing if the fire turns out to be minor, and saves minutes if it does not.
